# Gross Profit Margin — Reference Example

**Status:** Educational reference; independent learner exercise not recorded.

## What It Means
Gross profit margin measures the proportion of revenue remaining after cost of goods sold (COGS).

## Why It Matters
It helps assess product or service economics before operating expenses and other costs.

## When to Use It
Use when revenue and COGS are defined consistently for the same period. It does not measure net profitability.

## Formula
**Gross profit margin (%) = (Revenue − COGS) / Revenue × 100**

## Hypothetical Step-by-Step Example
Revenue = $10,000; COGS = $6,000.

1. Gross profit = $10,000 − $6,000 = $4,000.
2. Margin = $4,000 / $10,000 × 100 = **40%**.

## Expected Result
**40%**.

## How to Interpret It
For each dollar of revenue, $0.40 remains after the specified COGS, before other expenses.

## Business Application
Compare gross margins across product categories, with consistent cost allocation.

## Common Mistakes and Limitations
Confusing markup with margin; excluding relevant COGS; comparing unlike reporting periods; dividing by zero; treating gross margin as net profit.
