# Current Ratio — Reference Example

**Status:** Educational reference; independent learner exercise not recorded.

## What It Means
The current ratio compares current assets with current liabilities.

## Why It Matters
It is one indicator of short-term liquidity.

## When to Use It
Use with balance-sheet figures from the same reporting date and in conjunction with cash flow, asset quality, and industry context.

## Formula
**Current ratio = Current assets / Current liabilities**

## Hypothetical Step-by-Step Example
Current assets = $120,000; current liabilities = $60,000.

1. Divide 120,000 by 60,000.
2. Result = **2.0**.

## Expected Result
**2.0 times** (or **2:1**).

## How to Interpret It
The example business reports $2 in current assets for each $1 of current liabilities; this does not guarantee adequate cash liquidity.

## Business Application
Review short-term balance-sheet liquidity alongside receivables aging and cash flow.

## Common Mistakes and Limitations
Using mismatched dates; assuming inventory and receivables are immediately liquid; dividing by zero; interpreting one ratio without trends or context.
